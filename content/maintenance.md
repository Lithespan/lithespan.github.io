Standard maintenance follows the corresponding upstream support window. Lithespan rebuilds supported targets after relevant upstream changes, reruns qualification, and publishes updated evidence.

## Public stable releases

Every stable public target names its upstream version line, platforms, architectures, output formats, test suite, compatibility matrix, and end-of-support date. Artifacts and evidence remain free to download. Community support is best effort.

## Private maintenance

Paid agreements can add private targets, more operating systems or architectures, a defined rebuild cadence, regression reports, controlled distribution, response times, and performance or compatibility obligations.

Scope is priced by the combinations that must be rebuilt and retested: versions, platforms, architectures, workloads, and outputs.

## Extended Maintenance

At upstream end of support, customers can migrate to a qualified successor or purchase a time-bound extension. The agreement defines:

- Software version and upstream baseline
- Supported OS, libc, CPU, and output combinations
- Patch and vulnerability scope
- Compatibility and regression schedule
- Response obligations and end date

Availability depends on technical, licensing, and security feasibility. Each extension funds a specific engineering obligation rather than an open-ended support promise.

## Channel commitments

Edge and candidate builds establish whether a target can be built and qualified. Stable adds an owner, evidence, and a public support window. Customer and LTS channels add private scope and contractual accountability. The [release model](/release-model/) defines how the same payload moves between channels.
